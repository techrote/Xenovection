# FOUND-003 — Procedural matter laboratory (Draft/HOLD)

## Control surface
Presets 08–13 retain the original camera, exact source inspector, clocks and transport. Open Matter, select a region slot, enable/disable it, change mode, seed, population and material. F focuses the selected region; I isolates it; click near a visible region centre to select (not surface picking). The same three stable region slots can be placed independently. Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z undo/redo bounded region parameter edits; R resets the preset. G suppresses all generated matter and restores scientific inspection.

## Field and geometry contract
Field IDs: 0 vortex, 1 sink, 2 analytic divergence-free trigonometric curl component, 3 helix, 4 softened dipole, 5 scaled Lorenz, 6 source. Two fields are blended with local Gaussian falloff, independent origins and moving disturbance. The CPU reference performs bounded midpoint integration; streamline freezes field time and pathline advances it. GPU deformation uses a local analytical swirl/curl approximation, not repeated numerical integration. Reintegrate bakes current field bases into flow paths. Field edits to strength, mix and origin immediately affect GPU deformation, advection and optics but do not silently rebuild the curves. CPU integration time is deterministic for the given build, engine and seed; no promise of cross-platform bit identity.

The film representation has a shared strip parameter domain: the width fraction changes from 4.5% to 100% of the row spacing, continuously closing gaps between curved strips. Shell maps the same geometry to an open spherical surface, not a solid or watertight shell. Film geometry is fixed-topology GPU deformation; pores and growth are fragment masks, not topological remeshing. Numerical flow and analytic film are distinct region representations; no claim of a general transform between arbitrary integrated streamlines and films. Moving highlights are not particle trajectories; the separate 48-particle population per flow region is bounded fixed-step advection with capped catch-up. Reverse freezes advection, while independent analytical shader clocks reverse. Scene save stores bases, baked field state, selection and clocks, but particles restart on recall.

## SYNTH-002 extension
Existing typed modulation bus: `matter.region0.<parameter>`, `matter.region1.<parameter>`, `matter.region2.<parameter>`. All slots always register; scalar ranges are declared in laboratory.gd SPECS. Stable names do not encode a source ID. Continuous targets (condensation, shell, deformation, strength, origin_x/y/z, mix, pore_size, growth, thickness, roughness, absorption, opacity, line_width, hue, trails, placement, rotation, scale) use evaluated values. Discrete topology (mode, seed, count, field, secondary, integration, pathline) uses baked bases and requires rebuild, not audio-rate routing. Keep the existing five visual clocks, with filament controlling flow, displacement controlling film, normal controlling microstructure, sky controlling optical response and palette controlling hue. No second patch system. Up to 3 regions × 192 strips × 80 segments and 3 × 48 particles; each region is a single indexed surface, plus optional particle draw. No per-frame surface reconstruction. Independent fields are per region, so overlapping regions need matching field parameters to share a disturbance; no global field-object editor yet.

## Optical scope
RGB phase samples imitate film interference; view-angle, roughness, absorption and animated analytical reflection are artistic approximations, not calibrated wavelength transport, actual environment capture or screen-space refraction. Alpha blends are order dependent, especially when layers intersect; no volumetric integration, ray tracing, physical caustics or scattering. Fine flow ribbons use screen-space expansion; film strips are world-space and can alias at extreme zoom-out. Transparent film normal is estimated from derivatives.
